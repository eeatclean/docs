# How an analysis works

An analysis reviews one article in depth. It takes a few minutes, because EEATClean checks the facts live, at the moment of the analysis. Here is what happens in between.

## 1. Read the article

EEATClean reads the main text of your article. It leaves out menus, sidebars, comments and other parts of the page that aren't your content.

## 2. List the key claims

Next, it lists the claims a reader relies on: versions, prices, dates, standards, procedures, tool names and figures. These are the facts that change after you publish, or that may have been wrong from the start.

## 3. Search current sources

Then it searches the web for each important claim. It looks first for the page that owns the fact: the maker of the product, the public body, the standard body or the original author of a figure. Learn more in [Sources](sources.md).

## 4. Compare and decide

EEATClean compares what your article says with what these sources say today. A correction is proposed only when a source proves it. If no source settles a claim, EEATClean doesn't guess: it leaves the claim alone.

## 5. Check every correction again

Finally, each proposed correction goes through one more check. This time, the goal is to prove the correction wrong: is it about the same product, the same version, the same country, the same date as your article? If the proof is too weak, or if the correction goes further than what your article says, the correction is dropped.

In our own study of 23 articles, this last check removed 15 proposed corrections before they reached the report. [Read the study](https://eeatclean.com/blog/what-we-found-checking-our-own-articles.html).

## The result

You get one verdict per article and, when needed, a list of corrections with their sources. See [Verdicts and corrections](verdicts-and-corrections.md).

EEATClean analyzes each article in its own language. It never changes your website: you decide what to update, in your own words.

## Can it make mistakes?

Yes. That's why every correction comes with its source, so you can check it in a few seconds. We still recommend reviewing important decisions yourself, especially before you delete an article.
