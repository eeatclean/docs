# Sources

A correction is only as good as its proof. That's why EEATClean follows one rule: **no source, no correction.**

## Which sources count

EEATClean looks first for the page that owns the fact:

- the maker of the product or tool: release notes, documentation, pricing page;
- the public body or regulator: laws, official procedures, rates;
- the standard body: the current edition of a standard;
- the original author of a figure: the study or report it comes from.

When the official page isn't available, EEATClean can use another reliable page that states the same fact clearly, for example a recognized reference or a well-known publication.

## Which sources don't count

- **A blog or a website that repeats the fact.** It may be outdated too.
- **An AI answer.** It isn't a source, it's a summary that can be wrong.
- **A page about another product, version, country or date.** Close isn't enough.

## Why the official page comes first

Before opening EEATClean to others, we checked our own articles. Out of the 68 corrections that needed a source, 62 were proven by the official page of the product's maker or of the public body concerned. In other words, the proof is almost always out there. Someone just has to go and read it. [Read the full study](https://eeatclean.com/blog/what-we-found-checking-our-own-articles.html).

## Check it yourself

Every correction links to its source. Open it, find the passage, and check that it says what EEATClean reports. If it doesn't, tell us at [support@eeatclean.com](mailto:support@eeatclean.com). We read every report of a mistake, and it helps us improve the analyses.
