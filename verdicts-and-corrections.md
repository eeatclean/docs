# Verdicts and corrections

Every analyzed article gets a verdict. When something needs to change, the report lists the corrections, each with its source.

## The three verdicts

| Verdict | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Needs an update** | At least one fact is outdated or wrong. The report tells you what to change. |
| **Review or remove** | The article no longer serves your readers as it is. Read the report, then decide whether to rewrite it or remove it. |
| **Good to keep** | No correction is needed. The report confirms which facts are still right. |

## How serious is each correction?

Each correction has a level, so you know where to start:

| Level | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Major | A fact or step your reader relies on that is wrong today, for example a procedure that no longer works. |
| Moderate | A detail that is out of date and worth fixing. |
| Minor | A small inaccuracy or wording that promises a bit too much. |

## What a correction shows you

Each correction shows three things:

1. **What your article says**, quoted from your page.
2. **What is true today**, explained in plain language.
3. **The source** that proves it, with a link.

Open the source, check the fact yourself, then decide what to change. EEATClean doesn't write replacement text into your article: you update it in your own words.

## After you fix an article

- **Mark as fixed**: tell EEATClean you corrected the article. It then counts as up to date until its next analysis. You can undo it.
- **Rescan**: analyze the article again to confirm your changes. A rescan uses one analysis.

## Content health

Your dashboard shows a content health score: the average of your analyzed articles. An article with no correction counts 100. Otherwise, its score depends on its most serious correction: 85 for minor, 60 for moderate and 30 for major.

The score only appears from 10 analyzed articles, and stays marked as provisional until 30. With fewer articles, a single report would move it too much.
